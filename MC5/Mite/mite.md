description: Tiny, mischievous humanoids that waylay dungeon adventurers for fun and profit. Mites have hairless, warty skin from light gray to bright violet, triangular heads with bat-like ears and long hooked noses, and high-pitched twittery voices that convey only simple ideas. Males have a bone ridge down the skull and short goatee beards. They wear filthy stolen rags several sizes too large.
combat: Tries to catch lone travelers and stragglers with pit traps (1d6 damage), nets (save vs paralysis or be caught) and trip wires (Dexterity check or fall prone). Prone or netted victims are swarmed and clubbed (each club has a cumulative 2% chance of stunning, but only if the victim wears armor worse than splint mail). The mites bind the unconscious victim, drag him to their lair, tease him for 1d4 days, then get bored, stun him again, steal everything and dump him elsewhere in the dungeon.
habitat_society: Lairs are dozens of tiny interconnecting corridors above and below the dungeon's main passages, with entrances hidden behind carefully placed stones (check for secret doors). In mite tunnels larger creatures take -4 to attack and +4 to AC. At the center is a large, low-ceilinged chamber with a mite king (a fierce 1+1 HD warrior who bites for 1d4), 4d6 mite women (who bite for 1-2) and 4d6 non-combatant children. The chamber is filthy and strewn with captured weapons, armor and clothes. The king keeps the bright gems, which mites love, and may play with them anytime.
ecology: Mites hunt vermin and other pests and love iron rations. Most monsters see them as bite-sized snacks, and evil giants sometimes serve them as appetizers. They are fond of bones and sometimes drag the skulls of great monsters into their lair.
